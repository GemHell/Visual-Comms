This assignment asks you to critically analyze an example of visual culture that you are interested in using the concepts and terms from our elements and principles of design lectures. The Oral Presentation is a five-minute presentation supported with a slide show of visual examples that examine the use of design principles in print, digital graphics, or web design.

The subject matter of the presentation can come from many fields of visual communication: art, advertisement, video game design, mobile app design, billboards, subway advertisements, graphic design, website design, etc. You cannot use anime, screen shots from a streaming series, graphic novels, or compositions from film for your subject of analysis. The Oral Presentation counts as 15% of your overall grade. Check with your Lab Instructor for the scheduled date of your presentation.

The art of creating a well-organized and insightful oral/visual presentation is something that will help your career whether you are showing a pitch deck to a venture capitalist or giving an artist talk. Your presentation will be assessed on the clarity of the presentation (both visual and oral), how well the presentation is organized, how well the student applies concepts from the lectures in their analysis, and the quality of the graphic presentation of their slides.

Questions to consider when analyzing your image (not every design will have each of these elements below, choose the top ones that are most important to the composition you’ve chosen):

  1. Is there a sense of unity, hierarchy and variety in the image? If so, what design elements are creating that impression? What is the proportion of the sides of your 2D image, what are size relationships that exist within the image that create an underlying structure?
  2. Examine the use of line in the image. What is the tone, emotion, feeling of the line? What is the lines direction? What is the tool making the line? Is the line straight, curved, thick, thin? What is the quality of the line, is it jagged, smooth, or erratic? How many lines are there?
  3. Are there supporting design principles like scale, balance, rhythm, repetition, proximity or economy that you feel are being used in a compelling way? How?
  4. Are there shapes present in your image? If so, are they organic, biomorphic, geometric or a combination? Is there a sense of figure and ground in your image? How is it being achieved? Is there an intriguing use of negative and positive space? Do you feel your image is a descendent of the flat graphics or Plakastil movement?
  5. How is typography being used in your image? How is tracking, leading, kerning being used in the type? In what way do the ascender/descender, baseline, counter, aperture, and bowl of the letters communicating a particular feeling or mood? Is the typeface a serif, sans serif, script, display or symbol? Why do you believe this typeface was chosen by the designer, what does it communicate and does it create a sense of unity with the other parts of the image?
  6. How is color functioning in your image? What is the color scheme of the design? Consider putting your image into Adobe Color to analyze the relationships between the colors. How do the hue, saturation and brightness convey a particular mood (warm/cool), emotion or feeling? How is color being used to reinforce a meaning, express an attitude or create emphasis? Are there cultural connotations of particular colors in the image? Do the adjacent colors of an image change how we perceive them? Is color being used to create depth or the illusion of 3D space?
  7. How are patterns being employed in the image you’ve chosen? Are they being used to create a visual distinction between different elements in the composition? Are there textures being employed in the image? If so, what kind of surface quality do they suggest? How is repetition and variety being used in the pattern or texture? Is there a sense of rhythm being created by the pattern?
  8. Is there an underlying grid to the composition/website/image you are analyzing? If so, how is the grid functioning to provide and visual unity? What type of grid is being used (manuscript grid, column grid, modular grid, or hierarchical grid). If you are analyzing a website design, describe how many span units make up the columns sections of your site, try creating an overlay using photoshop to demonstrate how many columns make up the grid (see the ‘960 grid’ example in the lecture slides). If your example is from a book or magazine, make sure to draw our attention to the margins, columns, hanglines, gutters and markers.
  9. Is there an aesthetic movement that we’ve discussed (pictorialism, f.64 group, NYC street photography, Dada, surrealism, art nouveau, Psychedelic art, modernist, Bauhaus, post-modernist, P&D movement, etc.) that you feel your image is in conversation with? Why?
